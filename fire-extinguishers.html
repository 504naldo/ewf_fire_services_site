More Than a Tag

A tag on a fire extinguisher confirms it was inspected — but inspection quality matters. An extinguisher that is low on pressure, improperly mounted, blocked from access, or past its service life may fail when it's needed most.

Internal maintenance and hydrostatic testing intervals vary by extinguisher type and service history. Review the unit label and service records when planning work; a six-year maintenance interval is not universal. Used or damaged extinguishers also need assessment for appropriate service or replacement.

EWF provides clear documentation of each unit's condition, service history, and required follow-up — giving property managers and strata councils a complete record for AHJ inspections and insurance purposes.

1

Inventory & Assessment

Locate and inventory all extinguishers on the property. Check type, rating, placement, mounting, and service history.

2

Inspect Each Unit

Check pressure, tamper seal, pin, physical condition, and accessibility. Identify any units requiring service, recharge, or replacement.

3

Service, Recharge, or Replace

Perform the maintenance, hydrostatic testing or recharge applicable to each unit. Confirm service arrangements and assess damaged or expired units for replacement.

4

Tag, Document & Report

Apply updated compliance tags and provide inspection records, deficiency notes, and recommendations for AHJ-ready property documentation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often do fire extinguishers need to be inspected in BC?
Inspection, internal maintenance and hydrostatic testing requirements depend on the extinguisher type, service history, manufacturer instructions and applicable requirements. A six-year internal maintenance interval does not apply to every extinguisher. EWF can review your units and records to identify the service needed.
What happens during an annual fire extinguisher inspection?
EWF technicians check the extinguisher's pressure gauge, tamper seal, pull pin, mounting bracket, accessibility, physical condition, and tag. Any unit that fails inspection is flagged for recharge, maintenance, or replacement with a clear explanation of what is required.
How are recharge and replacement services arranged?
EWF can assess recharge, maintenance or replacement needs and provide service documentation. Before scheduling, confirm which work can be completed on site, which units may need workshop service and any replacement arrangements for your property.
